Outcome

What helped a stock recover from one crash didn't help in the next.

We trained models on each of three market crashes and tested them on the other two. Inside a crash, a firm's pre-crash financials predict recovery. On a new crash, the same models do no better than a coin flip.

The data covers 10,513 firm-crash rows built from SEC filings for every U.S. public filer, including firms that later failed, across the 2020 COVID-19 crash, the 2022 rate-hike bear market and the 2025 tariff shock.

Each square is the model's AUC: the chance it ranks a firm that recovered above one that didn't. 0.5 is a coin flip.

Three crashes with three different causes

COVID-19 was a deep, fast drop. The 2022 rate hikes were a slow grind that took over a year to recover from. The 2025 tariff shock was short and shallow. A signal that predicts recovery in all three would be more likely to be real than one tuned to a single event.

Inside a crash, financials beat a sector-and-size baseline

Within each crash, LightGBM gains 0.02 to 0.07 AUC over a baseline that knows only sector and size, and the bootstrap interval of that gain excludes zero every time. Trained on the other two crashes, the same model falls below the baseline: pooled over every cross-crash test it scores 0.055 lower for recovery (95% CI −0.066 to −0.044).

Why: the same signal points different ways in different crashes

Each crash has its own leading signals, and some reverse outright. In the 2020 liquidity panic the most volatile stocks fell hardest and bounced back hardest. In 2022 they recovered at half the rate of the calmest third. A model that learned "volatility helps" in 2020 applied that rule in 2022, where volatility hurt.

The best kind of firm to hold changed with the crash

k-means on the pre-crash financials finds three broad archetypes. They're loose groups (silhouette 0.15), but the archetype mix barely changes between crashes while each archetype's outcome changes a lot.

Profitable, cash-generating firms recovered most often in 2020. Large, calm firms led in 2022 and 2025. Loss-making firms fell furthest behind in 2022.

How the table was built

The universe starts from SEC filers, not today's listings, so firms that later failed stay in. Each row's features are financials filed before the market peak; its labels come from prices after the trough.

    No look-ahead

    Features use only SEC facts filed before the peak, so later restatements can't leak in. A test checks that no fact or price dated after the peak is used.

    No survivorship bias

    Dead firms come from SEC records and Tiingo, not current ticker lists. The 3.4% of matched rows still without prices fail more often, and are reported as a limit.

    Right company, right price

    Reused tickers are caught by matching price levels against SEC records and checking each ticker's issuer at the peak.

    One firm, three crashes

    71% of rows belong to firms in all three crashes, so cross-validation folds and bootstrap resamples keep each firm's rows together.
